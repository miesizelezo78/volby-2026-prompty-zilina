# Prompt for Comprehensive Automatic Comparison of Candidates – Žilina and Žilina Region (Elections 2026)

## Task (execute step by step and thoroughly)

1. **Search for the current candidates** for these two positions in the 2026 elections:
   - Mayor of the city of Žilina
   - President of the Žilina Self-Governing Region (župan)

   List names, age (if known), political support / who they are running for, and the source of the information.

2. For **each found candidate** (at least the main ones), perform an in-depth analysis according to the points below.  
   **It is not enough to list promises.** You must look for and report past activity, track record, values, and connections.

3. Finally, create a **comparison of all** including tables, spectra, and numerical ratings.

## Mandatory content of the analysis for each candidate

### A. Past activity vs. promises (by area)
For each relevant area, state:
- What they currently promise
- What they actually did in the past (councilor, committee member, mayor, entrepreneur, expert, public statements…)
- Difference / alignment between promises and track record
- If information is missing, write “unavailable”

**Areas for Mayor of Žilina:**  
housing and municipal land, transport and parking, safety, finances and transparency, education, social services, environment and waste, development of city districts, economic development.

**Areas for Regional President:**  
class II/III roads and bridges, regional transport, healthcare (regional hospitals), social services, secondary education, regional development and retaining young people, tourism, regional finances.

### B. Values and political orientation
- What values they have long promoted (transparency, family, social solidarity, support for business, environment, cultural identity, security, openness vs. conservatism…)
- On which specific positions or decisions this has been shown
- Political past and party affiliation
- Who / which current they lean towards (even if running as independent)
- Whether the value orientation is consistent

### C. Links to financial and business structures
- Only verifiable public sources (commercial register, contracts, media reports, asset declarations)
- Nature of the links (direct / indirect / media-mentioned)
- If none are documented, explicitly state it

### D. Communication and social media
- Style of presentation (concrete vs. slogan-based, polemical, marketing…)
- Frequency and content (last 12–24 months)
- How they respond to feedback / criticism

## Comparative outputs (mandatory)

### 1. Overview table
Markdown table: candidates in columns, key areas + track record in rows. Brief but factual cells.

### 2. Value / ideological spectrum
Place each candidate on these axes (in words + number 1–10):
- Economic (1 = strong regulation → 10 = free market)
- Socio-cultural (1 = conservative → 10 = progressive)
- Transparency and openness (1 = low → 10 = high)
- Investment priority (infrastructure / social / development)

### 3. Benchmark / radar (numerical scores)
For each candidate, rate 6–8 areas on a scale of **1–10** based on **past activity and verifiable facts** (not just promises).  
Format the output as a list that can be easily turned into a radar chart.

### 4. Strengths and weaknesses (comparison)
Briefly: in which areas each candidate is stronger / weaker according to the track record.

### 5. Final summary
Main differences, what to watch out for, and overall assessment of transparency and consistency.

## Strict rules

- **It is not enough to list promises.** Priority is past activity and verifiable facts.
- Separate facts, promises, and interpretations.
- If something is not publicly available, state it.
- Be factual, balanced, and concrete.
- Outputs must be comprehensive, not just two simple tables.

**Sources:** election lists, programs, council minutes, social media, media, interviews, commercial register, public contracts, asset declarations.
